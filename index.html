The weekly rhythm

Learn it, draw it, present it.

1. Watch & learn

Each week I introduce a new drawing type on video — what it is, what belongs on it, and how to build it — broken into short, followable segments.

2. Draw & ask

Students work through their assignment on their own during the week, posting questions and getting feedback from me along the way.

3. Present & get feedback

In our live class, every student presents their drawing for a few minutes and receives warm, constructive feedback — from me and from classmates.

Eight weeks, one drawing at a time

By the end, your student has drawn a full set of plans for a house they designed themselves.

01

Introduction to architectural plans

What types of drawings do architects actually use, and why? Students learn the architectural scale — the same tool used on real construction documents — and how to read it.

02

Site plan

Imagine your dream property — that's what gets drawn. Students learn the elements, scale, and symbols that belong on a site plan.

03

Floor plan

The drawing everyone pictures when they hear "architectural plans." Students design a unique floor plan from an inspirational object, and learn the dos and don'ts of laying out rooms.

04

Roof plan

The basic elements, scale, and symbols of a roof plan — plus the fundamental roof types and how to draw each one.

05

Exterior elevations

Students explore architectural styles and the door and window types that go with them, plus the materials homes are built from — and the trade-offs of each.

06

Sections

We look at the house like a dollhouse with the front wall removed, studying how rooms relate to each other vertically — and touch on basic structure.

07

Interior elevations

Time to play interior designer: students draw each wall of two rooms and consider finishes, flooring, wall materials, and furniture layout.

08

Choice drawing

Students pick their own finale — a perspective, a rendered floor plan or elevation, an axonometric, and more.

Learning outcomes

A real, working introduction to architecture.

  • How to use an architectural scale to draw and measure to scale
  • How to read the symbols used on real architectural plans
  • The basic types of architectural drawings and how they describe an actual building
  • How to present ideas to others and take in constructive feedback
  • A finished, hand-drawn set of plans for a house they designed themselves
  • A fun, exploratory first taste of what architecture school is actually like

What to have on hand

Simple tools. No software required.

Everything below is available at any art or drafting store, or on Amazon — the full set typically runs under $25.
  • Architectural scale for scaled drawings
  • 11×17 graph paper the drawing surface
  • Mechanical pencil keeps lines crisp
  • White high-polymer eraser won't tear the paper
  • 1/4"=1'-0" floor plan template furniture, fixtures, doors
  • Eraser shield optional, but genuinely fun
